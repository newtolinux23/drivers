02Chapter 02

Working on FedEx Ground routes

You work for C & S. C & S contracts with FedEx Ground. That means three rulebooks apply to you.

FedEx Ground does not employ its pickup-and-delivery drivers directly. It contracts routes to independent businesses — contracted service providers like C & S — who employ the drivers, own or lease the trucks and are responsible for safety compliance. You are a C & S employee, but you represent FedEx at every door.

1 · The lawFederal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations (49 CFR Parts 382–396), hazmat rules (Parts 171–177), OSHA, and your state and city traffic laws.
2 · FedEx Ground contracting standardsUniform and appearance, vehicle look and condition, safety technology (cameras), service standards and scanning procedures. FedEx can refuse a driver who does not meet them.
3 · C & S policyThis handbook: our 7-step day, call-first rules, truck care and conduct.

Safety technology in your truck

  • FedEx Ground requires contractors to run an approved video event data recorder (VEDR) — a road- and driver-facing camera that flags risky events like following too close, rolling stops, phone use and no seat belt.
  • Many trucks also have collision and lane-departure warnings, blind-spot and backing cameras. Treat alerts as real.
  • Blocking, unplugging or tampering with any camera or sensor is grounds for immediate termination.

We are scored every day

  • FedEx measures each contractor on safety and service: on-time pickups, delivery completion, exceptions, damages, and camera events.
  • Those numbers decide whether C & S keeps and grows its routes — and your job.
  • Every Code 27, every uncoded package and every camera event shows up on our scorecard.
03Chapter 03

Qualifications, records & testing

What you need to drive — and keep — a route.

To drive for C & S you must

  • Be at least 21 to drive a commercial vehicle across state lines. 49 CFR 391.11
  • Hold a valid driver's license for the vehicle you drive — and keep it valid.
  • Pass a DOT physical and carry a current medical examiner's certificate. It lasts up to 24 months, or less if the examiner says so. 49 CFR 391.41–391.45
  • Read and speak English well enough to understand road signs and talk with officers. 49 CFR 391.11
  • Pass a background check, a driving-record (MVR) review and a pre-employment drug screen.

Tell management immediately if

  • Your license is suspended, revoked or expires.
  • You get any traffic ticket or are convicted of a moving violation — in any vehicle.
  • Your medical card is about to expire, or a health change affects your driving or a medication makes you drowsy.
  • You are involved in any crash or damage, however small.
Your driver file

C & S keeps a federal driver qualification file on you (application, MVRs, medical card, road test). Your driving record is reviewed every year. Keep your copy of the medical card in your wallet — an inspector can ask for it.

04Chapter 04

Uniform & professional appearance

Customers open the door to a FedEx professional. FedEx Ground's contracting standards require it — and so do we.

The uniform

  • FedEx uniform shirt with the FedEx logo, tucked and clean.
  • Uniform pants or shorts, with dark shoes and dark socks.
  • Jackets, caps and cold-weather layers must be FedEx-approved uniform items. No hoodies or other brands over the uniform.
  • Keep every piece in good condition — no rips, tears or noticeable stains. Ask for a replacement before it gets there.

ID & grooming

  • FedEx ID badge visible at all times on route and at the terminal.
  • Your appearance should match your badge photo.
  • Clean, neat and well groomed — hair and facial hair trimmed, no body odor.
  • Tattoos or piercings management finds offensive must be covered or removed on duty.

Safety gear (C & S)

  • Closed-toe, slip-resistant work shoes or boots. No sandals, slides or bare canvas shoes.
  • High-visibility vest on the terminal yard and dock.
  • Gloves for heavy, sharp or wet freight.
  • No earbuds in both ears. Calls only hands-free, where your state allows it.
Why it matters

FedEx can refuse dispatch to a driver who does not meet its appearance standard. Out of uniform means off the route.

From the first walk-around to the last key in the lockbox — how a C & S professional runs a route.

05Chapter 05 · Step 1 of 7

Pre-trip inspection & readiness

Winning the day starts before you turn the key. Federal law says you may not drive until you are satisfied the truck is safe. 49 CFR 392.7 · 396.13

Walk-around

  • Last report: read the previous driver's inspection report. If it listed defects, sign that the repairs were made.
  • Tires: inflation, cuts, bulges, tight lug nuts. Minimum tread 4/32" on front steer tires, 2/32" on all others. 393.75
  • Brakes: firm pedal and a parking brake that holds.
  • Steering: no excess free play. On a 20" wheel the limit is 2½" (manual) or 5¼" (power steering). 393.209
  • Lights & glass: headlights, brake lights, turn signals, flashers, mirrors, wipers, horn and backup camera.
  • Fluids & leaks: oil and coolant levels, and no puddles under the truck.
  • Fuel: leave the station with a full tank.

Emergency equipment 393.95

  • Fire extinguisher rated 5 B:C (or two rated 4 B:C), charged, secured and within reach.
  • Three reflective warning triangles (or at least six fuses/flares).
  • First-aid kit, registration, insurance card and annual inspection proof.
  • 2026 update: spare fuses are no longer a federal requirement and liquid-burning flares are no longer accepted warning devices. Use triangles.

Scanner, GroundCloud & route

  • Scanner and spare battery fully charged and syncing.
  • Log in to GroundCloud with location tracking ON before you leave the terminal.
  • Review your manifest for stops or pickups outside your area, and tell dispatch before you roll out.
  • Report warning lights, tire problems or new damage at the dock — never drive a truck you think is unsafe.
06Chapter 06 · Step 2 of 7

Dock organization & preventing Code 27

Fifteen minutes on your shelves in the morning saves an hour of searching on the road.

Why it matters

  • Sort and lip-load every package by stop ID (SID) and sequence, label facing the aisle, before dispatch.
  • A Code 27 (did not attempt) usually means a package was buried or on the wrong shelf, missed at the stop and found too late.
  • Heavy freight goes low. Keep the center aisle clear — it is your walkway.

Catch problems at the dock

  • Misloads: packages for another route or ZIP code go back to the terminal before you leave.
  • Bad addresses: missing street, suite or apartment numbers get flagged so management can research them early.
  • Damage: crushed, wet, leaking or open boxes go to QA before loading.
  • Heavy packages: boxes over 70 lb usually carry a heavy-package label. FedEx Ground takes packages up to 150 lb — plan a hand truck or team lift.

Safe driving — accuracy over speed

Time is saved at the dock, never by speeding on the street.

100%Seat belt use
0Handheld phone use
6+ sFollowing distance
3Points of contact

Road rules

  • Obey every posted speed limit, including school zones and neighborhoods.
  • Seat belt on before the truck moves — even 50 feet between houses. 392.16
  • Full stops at every stop sign. Rolling stops are camera events.
  • Keep at least 6 seconds behind the vehicle ahead, and more in rain or at night.

Backing & parking

  • Park so you can pull forward. Avoid backing whenever possible.
  • If you must back up: G.O.A.L. — Get Out And Look, flashers on, two horn taps, then back slowly using mirrors and camera.
  • At every stop: parking brake set, engine off, keys out, doors locked when out of sight.

Your body

  • Three points of contact getting in and out of the cab and cargo area. Never jump out.
  • Lift with your legs, keep the load close and do not twist under weight.
  • Use the hand truck for heavy or bulky packages, and get help with anything you cannot handle safely.
08Chapter 08 · Step 4 of 7

Delivery execution & signatures

Right package, right door, right proof — every stop.

At the door

  • Read the label twice: once in the truck and again at the door. Check name, street number, street and apt/suite.
  • Upper-floor apartments: carry the package to the actual apartment door. Never leave it at the stairwell or breezeway.
  • Place it out of street view and out of the weather: porch, garage, side door or locker.
  • Sharp proof-of-delivery photo: the package in its spot, with enough of the doorway to show where. No people, no faces, no blurry shots or photos taken from the cab.
  • If you choose "Other" on the scanner, type exactly where you left it.

Gates, dogs & doubts

  • Locked gate or loose dog blocking the door? Find a safe, sheltered alternative spot — or call before you code.
  • Rattle the gate, honk or whistle before you enter any yard. See Chapter 15 for dog safety.
  • Not sure a spot is safe? Call Rob or Jamal from the stop before you walk away.

09Chapter 09 · Step 5 of 7

Pickups & 100% scanning

Pickups weigh heavily on our FedEx scorecard. They run on the clock.

Never early, never late

  • Review every scheduled pickup on your scanner and GroundCloud before you leave the station.
  • Arrive and scan inside the customer's pickup window. A scan before it opens or after it closes counts as a service failure.
  • Running late for a window? Call dispatch before you miss it, not after.

Scan everything

  • Scan each package as you load it. Never toss unscanned boxes in the back to scan later.
  • Nothing ready at a scheduled pickup? Still arrive inside the window, talk to the shipper and close the stop with the correct code.
  • Inspect hazmat pickups for proper paperwork and labels before you accept them (Chapter 14).

An honest, accurate code tells the customer the truth — and protects you.

Code 02 — call first

Only for a truly wrong or incomplete address. Never because you can't find the house. Call Jamal (347-527-0069) or Rob from the stop before you apply it.

Code 27 — prevent it

"Did not attempt" is almost always a dock problem. Sort by SID every morning, and call if an emergency blocks a stop.

Zero uncoded packages

Never bring a package back without a code. Walk the cargo area front to back before you return, then hand every return to management.

11Chapter 11 · Step 7 of 7

End of day — clean truck, clean handoff

Your truck is your office. Leave it the way you would want to find it.

Zero tolerance · urine bottles & human waste

Leaving urine bottles or any human waste in a cab or cargo area is a health hazard and disrespectful to the next driver and the mechanics. It results in immediate disciplinary action, up to and including termination. Need a bathroom stop? Take it — and tell dispatch if your route makes that hard.

Clean out

  • Remove wrappers, cups, bottles, shrink wrap, cardboard and trash from the dash, floor and shelves.
  • Leave the truck ready for the next shift.

Hand off returns

  • Every undelivered package has a valid code.
  • Hand returns to a manager with an explanation — never drop them in a bin unattended.

Post-trip report

  • Federal law requires a written report when you find or are told about a defect. 396.11
  • C & S requires a post-trip check every day. Report lights, brakes, tires or damage so repairs happen overnight.
  • Dock the scanner, charge batteries and put keys in the lockbox.

13Chapter 13

Phones, seat belts, alcohol & drugs

The fastest ways to lose your job — and your license.

Phones & screens 392.80 · 392.82

  • No texting while driving — that includes reading texts, emails and apps.
  • No handheld calls. Do not hold the phone, and do not dial by pressing more than one button.
  • Hands-free is allowed only if the phone is mounted within reach while you are belted in.
  • "Driving" includes sitting at a red light or in traffic. Pull over and park first.
  • Only exception: calling 911 or police in an emergency.
  • These violations carry heavy fines, and repeat offenses can disqualify you from driving commercial vehicles.

Alcohol & drugs

  • C & S policy: zero. No alcohol, marijuana or illegal drugs on duty, on station property or in a truck.
  • No alcohol within 4 hours before duty.
  • DOT standards: an alcohol level of 0.04 or higher is a violation, and 0.02–0.039 means at least 24 hours off duty. 382.201 · 382.505
  • State marijuana or medical cards do not count — federal transportation rules still apply.
  • Prescriptions that cause drowsiness: tell management before you drive.
  • Refusing a required test is treated the same as a positive test.
Testing

Every C & S driver is tested before hiring and when management has reasonable suspicion or after a crash. Drivers who hold a CDL are also in the federal random-testing program (49 CFR Part 382). Go to the clinic right away when directed.

14Chapter 14 · 49 CFR Parts 171–177

Hazardous materials & dry ice

Most hazmat you will see is small, legal and safe — if it is packed, papered and loaded right.

Before you accept or load

  • Check for hazard labels and markings: the UN number, proper shipping name, class and packing group.
  • Check the FedEx Ground hazmat shipping paper (OP-900) and the shipper's signed certification. They must match the package.
  • Leaking, damaged, unsigned or mislabeled? Do not load it. Code 83 and call management.
  • Limited Quantity packages show the black-and-white diamond mark and may weigh up to 30 kg (66 lb) gross. The old "ORM-D" marking is no longer valid for ground shipments.

In the truck

  • Keep shipping papers within reach while belted, or in the driver-door pouch when you are out of the cab. 177.817
  • Load hazmat near the rear door, upright (arrows up), blocked and braced. Never stack heavy freight on top.
  • Keep incompatible classes apart, such as corrosives (class 8) away from oxidizers (class 5).
  • Placard limit: 1,001 lb or more combined of placard-table hazmat requires placards and a hazmat-endorsed CDL. C & S P&D trucks stay below that limit. 172.504

Dry ice (UN1845, class 9)

  • Dry ice turns into CO₂ gas that pushes oxygen out of closed spaces.
  • The package must show the net weight in kg.
  • Load it in the cargo area only, never in the cab. Keep the bulkhead door closed and let air in before you climb in after a long stretch.

Never on a C & S P&D truck

  • Explosives (1.1–1.3), poison gas (2.3), dangerous-when-wet (4.3), poison-inhalation hazards, infectious substances (6.2) and radioactive material (class 7).
  • Damaged or recalled lithium batteries, or any battery shipment that isn't marked correctly.
  • In doubt? Stop and call.
15Chapter 15

Heat, dogs & personal safety

Your life is worth more than any package.

Beat the heat — water, rest, shade

  • Drink water often, before you feel thirsty — about a cup every 15–20 minutes in hot weather. Go easy on energy drinks.
  • Cool down in the A/C or shade on breaks. A cargo area in summer gets far hotter than the street.
  • New or returning from time off? Your body needs about 1–2 weeks to adjust. Pace yourself and take more breaks.
  • Wear light, breathable uniform layers and a FedEx cap.

Know the signs

  • Heat exhaustion: headache, dizziness, weakness, heavy sweating, nausea, cramps. → Stop, cool down, drink water and call dispatch.
  • Heat stroke (an emergency): confusion, slurred speech, fainting, seizures, very hot skin — sweating may stop. → Call 911, get to shade, cool with water and fanning.
  • Sweating does not mean you're safe. Watch your coworkers too.

Dogs

  • Look for bowls, toys, chains and "Beware of Dog" signs. Rattle the gate or honk before you enter.
  • A dog comes at you: do not run. Stand still, keep the package between you and the dog, avoid eye contact and back away slowly.
  • If you are bitten: get to safety, clean the wound, report it immediately and get medical care.

Robbery & threats

  • Never fight over a truck, scanner or freight. Comply calmly and remember details safely.
  • Get to safety, then call 911 and management.
  • If something feels wrong at a stop, leave and call. We will back your decision.
  • Ice, flooding or storms: slow down, and call if the roads aren't safe.
16Chapter 16

Accidents, breakdowns & emergencies

Stay calm. Follow the steps. Call us.

Stop and secure the sceneNever leave the scene. Flashers on immediately, parking brake set, engine off if there's any leak or smoke.
Check for injuries and call 911Help only as far as it's safe. Do not move injured people unless they're in danger.
Set out triangles within 10 minutesTwo-way road: 10 ft from the truck on the traffic side, 100 ft behind and 100 ft ahead. Divided or one-way road: 10, 100 and 200 ft behind. Near a hill or curve: put one 100–500 ft back so drivers see it in time. 392.22
Call management right awayRob ("Brower") or Jamal Sealy at (347) 527-0069 — before you talk to anyone else about fault.
Document, don't debatePhotograph every vehicle, plate, the damage, the road and the signs. Exchange license, insurance and registration. Be polite and don't admit fault — the camera and the facts will tell the story.
Testing & reportingIf directed to test, go immediately. Under federal rules, alcohol tests happen within 2 hours, drug tests within 32 hours, and you may not drink alcohol for 8 hours after the crash. Stay reachable. 382.303

Leaking or smoking package

  • Stop in a safe, open area away from people.
  • Don't touch it or breathe the fumes. Take the shipping papers and move upwind.
  • Call 911, then C & S management, then FedEx (1-800-463-3339).

Breakdown

  • Get fully off the road if you can. Flashers on, triangles out.
  • Call dispatch — the spare truck and a rescue driver are on the way.
  • Stay with the truck and freight unless you're unsafe.
