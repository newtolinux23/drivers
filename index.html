Step 1: Morning Pre-Trip Inspection & Readiness (At the Station)

Winning the day starts at your assigned station before you ever turn the key (49 CFR Parts 393 & 396).

🔧 DOT / FMCSA Pre-Trip Vehicle Walkaround

  • Full Fuel Tank Verification: Confirm your assigned truck has a full tank of gas/fuel before leaving the station.
  • Tires, Wheels & Rims: Inspect for proper inflation, cuts, bulges, and legal tread depth (≥ 4/32" on front steer tires; ≥ 2/32" on rear drive duals). Ensure all lug nuts are tight.
  • Brakes & Steering: Test service brake pedal firmness, parking/emergency brake hold, and check steering play (≤ 2 inches on a 20" wheel).
  • Lights, Mirrors & Visibility: Verify headlights, brake lights, turn signals, 4-way hazard flashers, clearance lights, reflectors, wipers, horn, and backup camera.
  • Fluid Levels & Undercarriage: Check oil/coolant levels and look underneath the vehicle for any active puddles or leaks.
  • Safety & Emergency Gear: Confirm a charged 5 B:C fire extinguisher, 3 reflective DOT triangles, first-aid kit, current registration, insurance card, and annual DOT inspection decal.

📱 Scanner, GroundCloud & Out-of-Area Check

  • Scanner & Battery Check: Confirm your Zebra/Forge handheld scanner and spare battery are 100% charged and syncing properly.
  • Mandatory GroundCloud Login: Log into GroundCloud on your tablet/device and verify that Location Tracking is turned ON before pulling out of the terminal.
  • Out-of-Area Itinerary Check: Review your scanner manifest and GroundCloud map before dispatch for any deliveries or pickups outside your assigned route or service area and notify Station Management / Dispatch immediately so they can be reassigned.
  • Report Defects Immediately: Report any mechanical warning lights, tire issues, or new body damage to management at the dock before dispatch.

Step 2: Morning Truck Organization & Preventing Code 27 (At the Dock)

How you set up your shelves in the morning determines your speed, accuracy, and stress level all day.

📦 Why Dock Organization Eliminates Code 27 (DNA)

  • Organize Every Shelf Before Dispatch: Sort and lip-load your packages by Stop ID (SID) and stop sequence before leaving the building.
  • What Causes a Code 27 (Did Not Attempt): A Code 27 happens when a driver arrives at a stop, cannot find the package because it is buried or on the wrong shelf, skips the stop, and discovers the package at the end of the shift when it is too late to deliver.
  • The Solution: Spending 15 extra minutes organizing your shelves at the dock saves 60+ minutes of searching on the road and eliminates preventable Code 27s.

🔍 Catch Misloads, Bad Addresses & Damages at the Dock

  • Warehouse Misloads (Code 12): Check labels while sorting for packages belonging to another route or wrong ZIP code and hand them back to station personnel before departure.
  • Questionable / Incomplete Addresses: Catch labels missing street numbers, suite numbers, or apartment numbers at the dock so management can research them early.
  • Damaged Boxes (Code 10): Inspect for crushed, wet, leaking, or open cartons and have QA tape or repackage them before loading.

Chapter 4 • Step 3 of 7

Step 3: Safe Driving Standards — "Accuracy Over Speed" (On the Road)

To be accurate, you do not have to speed. Time is saved at the dock—never by speeding on the street.

🚨 Zero Speeding & Seatbelt Mandate

  • No Speeding (Highways or Streets): Strictly obey posted speed limits on freeways, main roads, school zones, and residential streets.
  • 100% Seatbelt Compliance: Click your seatbelt before shifting into drive at every single stop—even when moving 50 feet between houses.
  • Zero Handheld Phone Use (49 CFR 392.82): Never hold, dial, or text on a cell phone while the vehicle is in motion.

📹 VEDR Camera & Backing Safety

  • VEDR Camera Integrity: AI-assisted Video Event Data Recorders monitor following distance (≥ 4–6 sec), rolling stops, distracted driving, and seatbelt use. Tampering with or covering a camera is grounds for immediate termination.
  • Park to Pull Forward: Avoid backing whenever possible. If you must reverse, use G.O.A.L. (Get Out And Look), tap the horn twice, activate 4-way flashers, and back at a crawling speed using mirrors and camera.

🦺 3 Points of Contact & Lifting

  • Three Points of Contact: Always maintain 2 hands and 1 foot (or 2 feet and 1 hand) on grab handles and steps when entering or exiting the cab or cargo bay. Never jump out of the truck.
  • Ergonomic Lifting: Keep packages close to your core ("Power Zone"), bend at the knees, never twist your spine under load, and always use your hand truck/dolly for packages over 70 lbs.
Chapter 5 • Step 4 of 7

Step 4: Package Delivery Execution, Locked Gates/Dogs & Signature Rules

Upholding the FedEx "Purple Promise" with double label verification, protected placement, and sharp POD photos.

🏠 Read Label Twice & Apartment Upper Floors

  • Read the Label Twice: Read the shipping label carefully inside the truck and a second time at the front door—verifying Recipient Name, Street Number, Street Name, and Apt/Suite #.
  • 2nd & 3rd Floor Apartments: Always walk packages up to the customer's actual apartment door on the 2nd or 3rd floor. Never dump upper-floor packages at the 1st-floor stairwell or breezeway entrance.
  • Sharp POD Photos: Take a crisp, well-framed photo showing the package in its sheltered location (never include people, faces, or reflections; never snap blurry ground shots or photos from inside the cab).
  • Cab & Cargo Security: Remove keys from the ignition at every stop and close/lock the bulkhead and rear doors when out of sight.

🐕 Locked Gates, Loose Dogs & Safe Placement

  • Dog Bite Prevention Protocol: Whistle, honk your horn, or rattle closed gates before entering any yard. Look for toys, bowls, or warning signs. If an aggressive dog approaches, DO NOT RUN—stay calm, avoid direct eye contact, use your package or clipboard as a shield between you and the dog, and back away slowly facing the animal.
  • Safe Weather-Protected Alternative Location: When a locked gate or loose dog blocks the front door, secure the package in a safe, sheltered spot (Porch, Garage, Driveway, or Package Locker).
  • Scanner Documentation ("Other"): Select the exact placement on your scanner. If selecting "Other", type a clear descriptive note telling the customer exactly where the package is located.
  • Call Management When in Doubt: Unsure if a spot is safe? Call Rob ("Brower") or Jamal Sealy (347-527-0069) from the stop before walking away.

Step 5: Strict Pickup Windows & 100% Individual Package Scanning

Pickups are heavily weighted on the FedEx Ground service scorecard and require exact clock discipline.

⏰ Hit Pickup Windows Strictly On Time

  • Check Your Pickup Schedule Every Morning: Review all assigned commercial and regular pickups on your scanner and GroundCloud before leaving the station.
  • Never Early, Never Late: You must arrive and open the pickup scan strictly inside the designated pickup window. Scanning even 1 minute before the open time or 1 minute after the close time triggers an automatic FedEx service failure.

📶 Scan 100% of Packages as You Load

  • Scan Every Individual Box: Scan every single package barcode as you load it into the truck—never bulk-toss un-scanned pickup packages into the cargo bay hoping to scan them later.
  • Zero Packages Ready (Code 22): If a scheduled pickup customer has zero outbound packages, still arrive inside the window, speak with the shipper, and close the stop with Pickup Code 22 (Nothing to Pick Up).

📞 STRICT CODE 02 RULE (CALL FIRST!)

Code 02 is ONLY for genuinely incorrect or incomplete addresses (missing street/apt #). NEVER use Code 02 just because you cannot find a house or building. You MUST call Jamal (347-527-0069) or Rob ("Brower") from the stop before applying Code 02.

⚠ MINIMIZE CODE 27 (DID NOT ATTEMPT)

Code 27 (DNA) happens when a package is buried on the wrong shelf and found too late. Organize your truck by SID at the dock every morning to keep Code 27 at zero. Call management immediately if an emergency blocks a stop.

🚫 ELIMINATE CODE 85 (ZERO UNCODED)

We do NOT want Code 85 inside the truck! A Code 85 occurs when a package is brought back to the terminal without any exception code scanned. Walk your cargo bay front-to-back before returning and physically hand all returns to management.

Step 7: End-of-Day Truck Cleanliness & Zero-Tolerance "Pee Bottle" Policy

Your truck is your professional office and a direct reflection of C & S Delivery Service, Inc.

☣ STRICT ZERO-TOLERANCE POLICY ON URINE ("PEE") BOTTLES

Leaving urine bottles ("pee bottles") or any human waste inside the cab or cargo bay is a severe OSHA/DOT biohazard, a health violation, and completely disrespectful to your fellow drivers and mechanics. No pee bottles are EVER allowed to be left in any truck. Violating this policy results in immediate disciplinary action up to and including termination.

🧹 Mandatory Daily Cab & Cargo Clean-Out

  • Remove all food wrappers, empty drink cups, water bottles, shrink wrap, broken cardboard, and trash from the dashboard, floorboard, and cargo shelves every evening.
  • Leave the cab and cargo bay spotless for the next morning's shift or rotator driver.

🤝 Physical Package Hand-Off at Station

  • Never toss undelivered packages into a bin unattended.
  • Every returned package must have a valid exception code scanned (Zero Code 85) and be physically handed to station management with a clear explanation before clocking out.

🔑 Post-Trip DVIR & Equipment Return

  • Complete your post-trip inspection and report any new mechanical issues, check-engine lights, brake wear, or tire/body damage to management immediately so repairs happen overnight.
  • Dock your scanner, charge batteries, and secure truck keys in the designated C & S lockbox.

⚠ Hazardous Materials (SF-920P & 49 CFR 177)

  • 999 lb Gross Aggregate Ceiling: Never load or transport 1,000 lbs (454 kg) or more of Table 2 hazardous materials on a single P&D van without proper placards and a CDL Hazmat endorsement.
  • Weight Caps: Max 70 lbs (32 kg) per Hazmat package (66 lbs / 30 kg for Limited Quantity / ORM-D cartons).
  • Paperwork Audit (OP-900LL): Before moving a Hazmat package, verify the OP-900LL multi-ply label and Shipper-Signed Certification match the 4-part sequence (UN/NA ID, Proper Shipping Name, Hazard Class, Packing Group). Keep Hazmat shipping papers in the driver's door pouch or within reach while belted.
  • Loading & Segregation: Load Hazmat on the rear floor near the roll-up door, blocked and braced against movement, with directional arrows pointing UP. Never stack heavy freight on top of Hazmat, and segregate incompatible classes (e.g., Class 8 Corrosives away from Class 4/5).

❄ Dry Ice (UN1845) & Prohibited Hazmat Classes

  • Dry Ice (Carbon Dioxide, Solid — Class 9, UN1845): Sublimates into CO2 gas that displaces oxygen in enclosed spaces. Verify the net weight in kg is marked on the diamond label, load in the rear cargo bay only (never inside the passenger cab), and keep the bulkhead door closed with cab air set to fresh/recirculated appropriately.
  • Strictly Prohibited in P&D Vans: Never pick up or load Explosives (1.1–1.3), Poison Gas (2.3), Dangerous When Wet (4.3), Poison Inhalation Hazard (6.1 PIH), Infectious Substances (6.2), Radioactive (Class 7), or unapproved Lithium Batteries (UN3090/UN3480). Apply Code 83 and call management.
Chapter 10 • Emergencies, Daily Checklist & Sign-Off

Emergency Protocols, Interactive Daily Checklist & Driver Sign-Off

Immediate accident/hazmat response, interactive daily readiness tracker, and official policy acknowledgment.

🚑 6-Step Accident & Breakdown Protocol

  • 1. Stop Immediately & Secure Scene: Turn on 4-way flashers, set parking brake, and place 3 DOT reflective triangles within 10 minutes (at 10 ft, 100 ft, and 200 ft). Never leave the scene of an accident.
  • 2. Call 911 & Management Immediately: Check for injuries, call 911 if needed, and call Rob ("Brower") or Jamal Sealy (347-527-0069) immediately from the scene.
  • 3. Document Without Admitting Fault: Be courteous, present your license/insurance/DOT cab card, photograph all vehicles, plates, and road conditions, and never debate fault at the scene.
  • 4. Post-Accident & Random DOT Testing (49 CFR 382): Any driver directed for a random or post-accident drug/alcohol test must proceed to the clinic within 2 hours. Refusal or delay equals a positive test.

☢ Leaking Hazmat & Active Threat Response

  • Leaking / Smoking Package: Stop in a safe open area, do not touch or inhale fumes, isolate the truck, keep shipping papers in hand, notify C & S Management and FedEx Emergency Response (1-800-463-3339), and wait upwind.
  • Active Threat / Armed Robbery: Your life comes first—never fight over a truck, scanner, or freight. Comply calmly, observe details safely, retreat to cover (GET OUT / HIDE OUT / TAKE ACTION), and dial 911 + management once safe.
