Section 01

Morning Pre-Trip Inspection & Readiness

At the Station

Winning the day starts at the terminal before you ever turn the key. Every driver is 100% accountable for the condition and readiness of their assigned truck.

  • 🔧 DOT / FMCSA Pre-Trip Vehicle Walkaround Before leaving the station, perform a full walkaround inspection. Verify that your truck has a full tank of gas/fuel and that all interior and exterior systems are working properly:
    • Tires, Wheels & Rims: Check for proper inflation, tread depth, cuts, or abnormal wear (including inner dual tires).
    • Brakes & Steering: Test service brakes, parking brake, and steering play.
    • Lights, Mirrors & Visibility: Verify headlights, brake lights, turn signals, hazard flashers, clearance lights, reflectors, windshield wipers, horn, and backup camera.
    • Fluid Levels & Undercarriage: Check fluid levels and look under the truck for any puddles or leaks.
    • Safety & Emergency Gear: Confirm the truck is equipped with a functional fire extinguisher, first-aid kit, and three reflective emergency triangles, plus your high-visibility vest, sturdy footwear, work gloves, and flashlight.
    • Report any mechanical issues or existing body damage to your Station Management immediately before dispatch.
  • 📱 Technology & Scanner Check Confirm your scanner and battery are fully charged and working properly. Log into GroundCloud and ensure your location tracking is turned ON before leaving the terminal.
  • 🗺️ Out-of-Area Check Review your scanner and GroundCloud itinerary for any deliveries or pickups outside your assigned service area and notify Station Management immediately so they can be reassigned.
Section 02

Morning Truck Organization & Preventing Code 27

At the Dock

How you set up your truck in the morning determines your success on the road and directly protects our company's service scorecard.

  • 📦 Organize Every Shelf & Zone Before Dispatch Take the time to fully sort and organize your packages by stop and shelf space before leaving the station. Secure heavy cargo so it does not shift in transit.
  • 🎯 Why Organization Matters (Eliminating Code 27) A Code 27 (Did Not Attempt) happens when a driver arrives at a stop, cannot find the package because it is buried or in the wrong spot, skips the stop, and then finds the package at the end of the day when it is too late to deliver. Organizing your truck before leaving the terminal ensures every package is in its proper place when you need it—saving you time and minimizing Code 27s.
  • 🔍 Catch Misloads, Bad Addresses & Damaged Boxes Early While organizing your load at the dock, actively look for:
    • Warehouse Misloads: Packages belonging to another route or zip code.
    • Questionable / Incomplete Addresses: Labels missing street numbers or apartment numbers.
    • Damaged Packages: Boxes that are crushed, open, or need repackaging.
    • Hand these back to the terminal immediately so they do not hurt our service rating.
Section 03

Safe Driving Standards — Accuracy Over Speed

On the Road

Safety is our highest priority. You do not need to drive fast to be an efficient, top-tier driver.

  • 🛑 No Speeding (Highways or Residential Streets) Strictly obey all posted speed limits on highways, main roads, and residential streets. To be accurate, you do not have to speed. Time is saved by organizing your truck at the dock and knowing your route—never by driving fast or recklessly.
  • 🦺 100% Seatbelt Compliance Always wear your safety belt whenever the vehicle is in motion—no exceptions.
  • 📵 Zero Phone Distractions (FMCSA 392.82) Keep handheld phones out of your hands while the truck is moving.
  • 👀 Defensive Driving & Backing Safety Maintain a safe following distance, check your mirrors every 5–8 seconds, watch for pedestrians, and park where you can pull forward whenever possible. If you must reverse, back up at a crawling speed and check your camera and mirrors.
  • 🪜 Three Points of Contact & Ergonomic Lifting Always use three points of contact (two hands and one foot, or two feet and one hand) when entering or exiting the truck—never jump out. Lift heavy boxes with your legs (bending at the knees, never twisting your torso) and always use a dolly/handcart for oversized loads.
  • 🚨 Weather, Fatigue & Emergency Protocol Stay hydrated, dress appropriately for heat or cold, and take breaks if you feel fatigued. In the event of any accident, injury, or property damage, stop immediately, call 911 if needed, and contact your Station Management / Dispatch right away—never leave the scene.
Section 04

Package Delivery Execution & Label Verification

At the Stop

Every package must be delivered accurately on the first attempt with complete care and verification, upholding FedEx's "Purple Promise" and C & S Delivery Service, Inc. standards.

  • 🏷️ Read the Label Twice Always read the shipping label carefully before stepping out of the truck and again at the doorstep. Verify the recipient's name, street number, street name, and apartment/suite number to guarantee an accurate delivery.
  • 🚪 Front Door & Protected Placement The customer's front door is the primary target—including walking packages up to the 2nd or 3rd floor of apartment complexes (never leave upper-floor packages at the 1st-floor stairwell).
  • 🐕 Handling Obstacles (Locked Gates / Loose Dogs) If the front door is inaccessible due to a locked gate, loose dog, or other hazard:
    • Dog Bite Prevention: Always check fenced yards for signs of dogs (honk your horn or rattle the gate before entering). If an aggressive dog approaches, do not run—stay calm, avoid direct eye contact, and back away slowly facing the dog.
    • Safe Alternative Placement: Place the package in a safe, weather-protected alternative location (such as the Garage, Porch, Driveway, or Package Locker).
    • Scanner Documentation: Select the exact placement on your scanner. If you select "Other", type a clear note telling the customer exactly where the package is secured.
    • When in Doubt, Call Management First: If you are unsure how or where to safely leave a package, call your Station Management or Dispatch Team immediately before leaving the stop.
  • 📸 Clear Proof of Delivery (POD) Photos Take a sharp, well-framed photo showing the package in its secure delivery spot. Never take blurry photos, photos of the ground, or photos from inside the truck cab.
  • ✍️ Signatures & Door Tags When a signature is required, collect it from an actual person at the address. Never falsify a delivery attempt from the truck. If no one is home, always leave a completed door tag on the door.
  • 🔒 Truck & Cargo Security Never leave keys in the ignition when stepping out of the cab, and keep the bulkhead and rear doors closed and locked when out of sight to prevent theft.
Section 05

Strict Pickup Windows & 100% Package Scanning

Clock Precision

Pickups are a critical part of our FedEx service scorecard and require strict clock management.

  • ⏰ Hit Pickup Windows Exactly On Time Check your scheduled pickups early in the shift. You must arrive and pick up packages strictly within the designated time window—never before and never after. Early or late scans trigger automatic service failures.
  • 🔫 Scan Every Single Package You must scan every individual package as you load it into the truck. Never throw un-scanned pickup packages into the cargo area.
Section 06

Exception Coding Rules — Code 02, Code 27 & Code 85

Service Accountability

C & S Delivery Service, Inc. is graded strictly on service numbers and accountability. Every code you enter either protects or hurts the entire team.

  • ✅ Use Only Legitimate Exception Codes Only apply codes that truthfully match the situation at the stop:
    • Code 04: Business closed during normal hours.
    • Code 07: Residential customer unavailable when a signature is required or residential access is blocked (must leave a door tag).
    • Code 11: Business closed on weekends.
  • ⚠️ Strict Code 02 Rule (Incorrect Address ONLY — Never "Cannot Locate")
    • Code 02 is strictly for genuinely incorrect or incomplete addresses (e.g., missing street number, missing apartment number, or non-existent street number).
    • Never apply Code 02 simply because you cannot find the house, building, or apartment complex.
    • Mandatory Call Protocol: Before using Code 02, double-check the label and apartment map. If you still cannot locate the address, you must call your Station Management or Dispatch Team from the stop before applying Code 02. Management will assist you with master maps or customer contact.
  • 📉 Minimize Code 27 (Did Not Attempt) Avoid Code 27 and Code 01 at all costs. By organizing your truck in the morning, you ensure you do not discover overlooked packages at the end of the shift. If an emergency prevents a delivery attempt, you must call Station Management immediately.
  • 🚫 Eliminate Code 85 (Zero Uncoded Packages Left in Truck)
    • We do NOT want Code 85 inside the truck. A Code 85 (or uncoded return) is triggered when a package is left behind in the truck at the end of the day without any exception code scanned on it, which severely hurts our service rating.
    • Before returning to the station, inspect your cargo area from front to back and ensure every single remaining undelivered package is properly coded.
  • 🤝 Physical End-of-Day Package Hand-Off Do not toss undelivered or coded packages into a bin unattended. Every returned package must be physically handed to your Station Manager with a clear explanation before you clock out.
📞
Remember the Golden Rule on the Road: Whenever you cannot find an address or have any doubt about how to code or leave a package, call your Station Management / Dispatch Team first while you are still at the stop.
Section 07

End-of-Day Truck Cleanliness & Post-Trip Protocol

Zero Tolerance

Your truck is your professional workspace and a reflection of C & S Delivery Service, Inc. We emphasize keeping our fleet spotless every single day.

  • 🧹 Mandatory Daily Clean-Out Before finishing your workday, thoroughly clean the cab and cargo area. Remove all trash, food wrappers, empty cups, plastic wrap, and debris so the truck is spotless for the next morning.
  • 🔑 Post-Trip Equipment & Maintenance Report Securely return or store all scanners, batteries, and truck keys as directed by Station Management, and report any new mechanical issues, warning lights, or tire/body damages discovered during the day so the truck can be repaired overnight.
☣️
STRICT ZERO-TOLERANCE POLICY ON "PEE BOTTLES":
Leaving urine bottles ("pee bottles") inside the cab or cargo area is a severe biohazard, a health violation, and completely unacceptable. No pee bottles are ever allowed to be left in the trucks. Violating this rule will result in immediate disciplinary action.
